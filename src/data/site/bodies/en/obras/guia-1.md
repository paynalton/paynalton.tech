I think I'm going to leave a copy of each one on my blog in case<br>someone wants to take a tour through them.<br><br>Let's start then:<br><br>**Why does my friend Linuxero get angry if I just say Linux and not GNU/Linux?**<br><br>You've surely<br>come across comments in forums or people who directly tell you:<br>"the correct name is GNU/Linux, if you say just Linux then<br>you're only referring to the kernel."<br><br>To me personally, it<br>seems a bit ridiculous to be so demanding about names, but<br>these attitudes have their reasons that go back to the principles<br>of our beloved penguin.<br><br>**The Story Behind Those Three Letters: GNU**<br><br>It<br>is said that in the beginning there was man, and for a while he was<br>good. Then man created computers in the image and likeness of his<br>television. jejeje.<br><br>What is counted, however, is that at the<br>beginning of the computer age, the real business was not the software,<br>but the computer itself. The software was written by any user<br>with moderate knowledge, and computing professionals freely shared<br>their creations, which quickly spread around the world and<br>achieved that phenomenal technological development that has characterized<br>computing.<br><br>But then companies started to realize that<br>software was something from which they could gain profit, and they<br>began to create restrictive rules so that their employees would<br>not disclose their developments and so that the companies could sell them.<br><br>These<br>attitudes were spreading throughout all the companies and the<br>information stopped flowing. A systems expert named Richard Stallman,<br>who was used to sharing and receiving information freely,<br>was working in a company that had just purchased a new printer.<br><br>That<br>printer wasn't working as the company needed, and, as a<br>good expert, Ricardito saw that the problem was in the printer's<br>controller.<br><br>So, just as he had done on other occasions,<br>he picked up the phone and asked how much they would<br>charge him for providing the source code of the driver, since he<br>wanted to make a correction, which he would later send to the manufacturer<br>to consider adding to their future versions. But his surprise<br>was huge when they told him they couldn't deliver the source code,<br>not even for sale.<br><br>After causing a telephonic revolt, gathering<br>great courage, and ending up with not a single line of code, Stallman<br>realized that around him companies were already protecting their source<br>code and simply thought: "This is no longer good."<br><br>By discussing<br>with other experts, they decided to bring an end to all of<br>this, so they created the FSF (Free Software Foundation), the<br>GPL licenses, and began working on a new operating system<br>filled with completely free applications and drivers. This system would<br>be named GNU (GNU is Not Unix).<br><br>It was the year of 1992 and<br>the system was already very complete, with hundreds of applications, but the<br>most important thing was missing, the core.<br><br>Although the system<br>wasn't completely finished, many wanted to use it already, but<br>without that core it was impossible to do so. Then came a<br>blessing in the form of a core from Linus Torvalds, who had<br>developed a core which he called Linux, which had been conceived<br>initially to be used in Minix systems.<br><br>However, it wasn't<br>very difficult to integrate it into the GNU system, and, many years before<br>it was expected, the first usable version of GNU, called GNU/Linux, emerged.<br><br>It<br>should be noted that GNU/Linux is not the pinnacle of success, since<br>GNU and Linux are independent projects that support each other<br>only. The main goal of the FSF is to have its own kernel, of which<br>we already have prototypes under the name of Hurd.<br><br>Then GNU/Linux is a word that tells us that we are looking at the GNU operating system using the Linux kernel.<br><br>I will<br>conclude by mentioning that at some point the native core of GNU will be<br>completed and promises major improvements over the current Linux kernel, which,<br>since its creation, has also never stopped improving.<br><br>However,<br>enjoy GNU/Linux for now knowing that thousands of people<br>are working to improve it. Although the system<br>currently does impressive things, when the GNU kernel is<br>finished, you'll see what a truly robust, stable,<br>intelligent, and capable system is.<br>
