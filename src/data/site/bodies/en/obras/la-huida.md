Talking with her, making faces of horror at the old woman's statements, is her neighbor, that one who in the middle of the night knocked on that door which now they try to break down, covered by a blanket in search of a way to prevent her husband from finding out that in that flaccid womb she carried the child of another man.<br><br>Some men have arrived with axes, among them the witch recognizes her faithful friend, the one who swore to protect her as a thank you for saving her wife from death. The priest shouts out instructions while calling Paozelieth a witch, monster, lover of Satan.<br><br>Beneath the tree the judge from the capital watches with satisfaction, believing that soon he will have a new slave to torture, violate, and burn alive. But surely he has never met a real witch before.<br><br>The law prevents Paozelieth from using her powers to harm anyone, alive or dead, so she will escape to start a new life. But she does not want to leave alone; she will wait until the last moment before departing, hoping that the one who once swore her love will come to her, fulfilling his promise of never abandoning her.<br><br>There he is, among the crowd. He will try to enter first, she knows this because she knows him better than anyone. The priest and a score of men are already crossing through the door of her room.<br><br>With the vial in her hand, the witch watches with hatred those familiar faces that now appear completely unknown. Pushing through them, a young man makes his way to the front of the crowd.<br><br>As he sees him, Paozelieth's fierce gaze loses the ferocity it had kept frozen on those men. Hope, gratitude, and love flood her heart. The young man, terrified, takes a few steps forward.<br><br>With a smile, Paozelieth extends her hand, inviting him to come closer.
